# US Individual Tax Positioning — 2026 (Forward-Looking, Not Filing)

**Researched:** September 25, 2026. All figures are for **tax year 2026** (returns filed early 2027) unless noted.
**Sources:** IRS newsroom releases, Revenue Procedure 2025-32 (IR-2025-103), IRS Notice 2025-67, Revenue Procedure 2025-19, OBBBA statutory text as summarized by IRS/CPA sources. Secondary sources (Kitces/NerdWallet/TurboTax/CPA firms) used only where they cite the IRS documents above.

> **This is general educational information, not personal tax advice.** Tax law is scenario-specific. Anything involving multi-state income, business entities, AMT, or >$150K incomes should be reviewed by a CPA or Enrolled Agent before acting.

**Why this matters for a money app:** The average 2026 filer got a **$3,275 refund** — an interest-free loan to the IRS. The EITC pays up to **$8,231** and ~20% of eligible workers never claim it. New 2025–2028 deductions (tips, overtime, car-loan interest, senior bonus) live on a brand-new Schedule 1-A that most filers have never seen. These are the highest-dollar "found money" levers in personal finance, and almost all of them are *positioning* decisions made during the year — not filing decisions made in April.

## 1. W-4 Accuracy: the fastest cash-flow change available

### How under/over-withholding happens
The post-2020 Form W-4 has no "allowances." Withholding is computed from: filing status (Step 1), a multiple-jobs adjustment (Step 2), dependent/credit dollar amounts (Step 3), other income (Step 4a), deductions (Step 4b), and extra withholding per paycheck (Step 4c). Withholding goes wrong when life changes but the W-4 doesn't: new job, second job, marriage/divorce, a child aging out of credits, starting gig income, or — new for 2026 — expecting the OBBBA deductions (tips, overtime, car-loan interest, senior bonus) that lower actual liability but aren't reflected in payroll tables.

### The multiple-jobs trap (the #1 cause of under-withholding)
IRS withholding tables treat **each job as if it were your only income**. Two $40K jobs withhold as if you earn $40K total, not $80K — so too little is withheld and you owe at filing, possibly plus penalty. Fix: check the Step 2(c) box on **both** W-4s (splits brackets/deduction across jobs; most accurate when pay is similar), or enter expected other income in Step 4(a) on the higher-paying job's W-4.

### The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator
Free at **irs.gov/W4App**. The IRS updated it in **March 2026** for the OBBBA: it now has fields for tips, overtime, and car-loan interest deductions and the senior deduction, and it auto-factors the higher 2026 standard deduction. Takes ~10 minutes with your latest pay stub. It outputs a filled-in W-4 to hand to payroll — effective the next pay cycle.

### When to file a new W-4
New job · second job starts/ends · marriage or divorce · child born or ages out of the CTC · gig/self-employment income starts · you expect large Schedule 1-A deductions (tips/overtime) · you got a refund >$1,000 or owed >$1,000 last year. Recheck mid-year at minimum.

### The cash-flow math
Per the National Taxpayer Advocate's 2026 mid-year report: **90.4M refunds, average $3,275**, and ~65% of returns got refunds. A $3,275 average refund is **~$273/month** of your own money lent to the IRS at 0%. At a 4% savings rate that's roughly **$65–$70/year in forgone interest** for the average refunder — plus the liquidity cost of not having the cash when a bill hits. Fixing a bad W-4 is the single fastest legal cash-flow change an employed person can make: it shows up in the next paycheck.

### The under-withholding side
Owe >$1,000 at filing without meeting a safe harbor and you pay the underpayment penalty — currently **~6%/yr interest** on the quarterly shortfall (see §3). The Estimator prevents both directions.

## 2. Credits people leave on the table

### Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) — up to $8,231
2026 figures (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). **Both** earned income and AGI must be under the limit; investment income must be **≤ $12,200** (hard cutoff — $1 over kills the whole credit).

| Qualifying children | Max credit | Max income: Single/HoH | Max income: MFJ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | $664 | $19,540 | $26,820 |
| 1 | $4,427 | $51,593 | $58,863 |
| 2 | $7,316 | $58,629 | $65,899 |
| 3+ | $8,231 | $62,974 | $70,244 |

- **Refundable** — paid even with zero tax liability. Claimed on Schedule EIC.
- The credit phases in with earnings, plateaus (largest credits go to workers earning ~$15K–$25K), then phases out.
- **The miss:** ~20% of eligible workers never claim it, mostly because **you must file a return to get it** — even if your income is below the filing threshold. Free filing: IRS Free File + VITA sites. Use the **IRS EITC Assistant** to check eligibility.
- No-children EITC: ages 25–64, can't be a dependent or full-time student under 24 in the usual sense — narrow but real.

### Child Tax Credit — $2,200/child (+$1,700 refundable)
- **$2,200** per qualifying child **under 17** at year-end (confirmed for 2026 by Rev. Proc. 2025-32; inflation indexing began 2026 but rounding to the nearest $100 didn't move it yet).
- **Additional CTC (refundable):** up to **$1,700**/child = lesser of remaining credit or 15% of earned income above **$2,500**.
- **$500** Credit for Other Dependents (17+, college students, elderly parents) — never refundable.
- Phase-out: MAGI over **$200K** (single/HoH/MFS) or **$400K** (MFJ); reduced $50 per $1,000 over. Thresholds are statutory, not indexed.
- **NEW & commonly missed:** starting with 2025 returns, the *claimant* (and at least one spouse on a joint return) must have a **valid SSN** — ITIN-only filers can no longer claim it. Tax Policy Center estimates ~500,000 children lost access. Earned income ≥ $2,500 required for any credit.
- Claimed on Schedule 8812.

### Child & Dependent Care Credit — EXPANDED for 2026 (permanent)
The OBBBA permanently raised this starting 2026 — the biggest change to it in decades:
- Expense caps: **$3,000** (one qualifying person) / **$6,000** (two or more).
- Credit rate now **up to 50%** (was 35%): 50% at AGI ≤ $15,000 → slides 1 point per $2,000 of AGI down to 35% at ~$43K → 35% plateau to $75K single / $150K joint → slides to a **20% floor** above ~$103K single / ~$206K joint.
- **Max credit: $1,500 (one) / $3,000 (two+)** — up from $1,050/$2,100.
- **Nonrefundable**; claimed on **Form 2441**; qualifying expenses can't exceed your (or spouse's) earned income; care must enable work/school.
- Companion change: the **Dependent Care FSA (DCAP) exclusion rises to $7,500** ($3,750 MFS) in 2026 — if your employer offers it, pre-tax payroll set-aside usually beats the credit.
- The miss: families who stopped tracking care receipts post-2021, and anyone not comparing DCAP vs. credit.

### Saver's Credit — up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint)
For low/moderate earners contributing to 401(k)/403(b/457/IRA/Roth IRA/ABLE. **Nonrefundable** (Form 8880). Must be 18+, not a full-time student, not a dependent.

| Credit rate | MFJ | Head of Household | Single/MFS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | AGI ≤ $48,500 | AGI ≤ $36,375 | AGI ≤ $24,250 |
| 20% | $48,501–$52,500 | $36,376–$39,375 | $24,251–$26,250 |
| 10% | $52,501–$80,500 | $39,376–$60,375 | $26,251–$40,250 |

- Credit = rate × first **$2,000** of contributions ($4,000 MFJ) → max **$1,000/person**.
- The miss: moderate earners who contribute to a 401(k) but never file Form 8880. Stacks with the EITC.
- **Forward note:** SECURE 2.0 replaces/augments this with the federal **Saver's Match** (government matching contribution deposited to your retirement account) for contributions **starting in 2027** — details still being finalized by Treasury; flag for 2027 planning.

### Education credits
- **American Opportunity Tax Credit:** up to **$2,500**/student/yr (100% of first $2,000 + 25% of next $2,000 of qualified expenses). **40% refundable** (up to $1,000). First 4 years of post-secondary, at least half-time, degree-seeking, no felony drug conviction. Form 8863.
- **Lifetime Learning Credit:** up to **$2,000**/return/yr (20% of first $10,000). **Nonrefundable**, unlimited years, no half-time requirement — covers grad school and professional courses.
- Both phase out at MAGI **$80K–$90K** single / **$160K–$180K** MFJ (not indexed for 2026). Can't claim both for the same student in one year. **New:** SSN required for the student (post-2025).
- The miss: grad students and career-switchers who don't know the LLC exists; families splitting AOTC across multiple undergrads (it's per-student).

### EV / clean-energy credits — DEAD (flag stale advice)
- New and used **clean vehicle credits terminated for purchases after Sept 30, 2025**. Home energy credits (solar, etc.) ended **Dec 31, 2025**. EV-charger credit ends after **June 30, 2026**.
- Any article, dealer, or "tip" promising a federal EV credit in 2026 is outdated. Do not build product logic around them.

## 3. Quarterly estimated taxes (gig / freelance income)

### Who must pay
Anyone who expects to owe **≥ $1,000** at filing after withholding and refundable credits — classic for 1099/gig workers, landlords, and W-2 workers with big side income. Separately, **Schedule SE is required at just $400** of net self-employment earnings (statutory threshold, unchanged since 1990) — many people who owe zero *income* tax still owe *SE* tax.

### 2026 due dates
| Quarter | Income period | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | **Apr 15, 2026** |
| Q2 | Apr 1 – May 31 | **Jun 15, 2026** |
| Q3 | Jun 1 – Aug 31 | **Sep 15, 2026** |
| Q4 | Sep 1 – Dec 31 | **Jan 15, 2027** |

Note the quarters are uneven (Q2 is 2 months, Q4 is 4). Uneven earners should use the **annualized income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI)** instead of dividing by four.

### Safe harbors (meet any one → no underpayment penalty)
1. **90% of current-year** total tax, paid across the four quarters; or
2. **100% of prior-year** total tax (**110%** if prior-year AGI exceeded **$150,000**, or $75,000 MFS); or
3. You **owe <$1,000** after withholding and refundable credits.
Most freelancers use #2 — the number is known on day one.

### The penalty rate (2026)
The penalty is **interest on each quarterly shortfall** from its due date until paid — federal short-term rate **+ 3 percentage points**, compounded daily, reset quarterly under §6621: **Q1 2026 = 7%** (Rev. Rul. 2025-22), **Q2 2026 = 6%** (Rev. Rul. 2026-5). Catching up stops *future* accrual but doesn't erase the period already late.

### How to pay
Form 1040-ES vouchers, **EFTPS** (enrollment required), **IRS Direct Pay** (bank account), or IRS2Go. Keep a simple ledger: income − business expenses = net → that's what SE tax and estimates are computed on.

### The gig-worker trap (dollar magnitude)
SE tax is **15.3%** (12.4% Social Security to the **$184,500** wage base + 2.9% Medicare, no cap; +0.9% additional Medicare tax above $200K/$250K) applied to **92.35%** of net earnings — on top of income tax. Half of SE tax is deductible (Schedule 1). Rule of thumb: **set aside 25–30% of every freelance dollar** the day it arrives. A $40K side profit ≈ $5,650 SE tax + ~$3–4K income tax — the most common "surprise bill" in the country. OBBBA made the **20% QBI deduction permanent** and raised the **1099 reporting threshold to $2,000** (single-sourced — verify before productizing).

## 4. HSA, 401(k), IRA — 2026 limits and timing

### HSA (Rev. Proc. 2025-19) — the triple tax advantage
- 2026 contributions: **$4,400** self-only / **$8,750** family (employer + employee combined).
- **+$1,000 catch-up** at 55+ (not on Medicare); if both spouses are 55+, total can reach **$10,750** (spouse's $1,000 must go in their own HSA).
- HDHP required: min deductible **$1,700**/$3,400; max out-of-pocket **$8,500**/$17,000 (2026).
- Triple advantage: deductible going in → tax-free growth → tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical. **Deadline: April 15, 2027** for 2026 contributions. After 65, non-medical withdrawals = ordinary income tax only, no penalty (a "stealth retirement account").
- **Traps:** a general-purpose FSA disqualifies you; excess contributions = **6% excise tax every year** the excess remains; non-medical withdrawal before 65 = **20% penalty** + income tax.
- Magnitude: maxing an $8,750 family HSA at the 22% bracket ≈ **$1,925** federal + ~$670 FICA (if via payroll) ≈ **$2,600/yr**.

### 401(k) / 403(b) / 457 / TSP (IRS Notice 2025-67)
- Employee deferral: **$24,500**. Catch-up 50+: **$8,000** (total **$32,500**). **Super catch-up ages 60–63: $11,250** (total **$35,750**). Combined employee+employer cap: **$72,000**.
- **NEW 2026 (SECURE 2.0):** if your 2025 wages exceeded **$150,000**, catch-up contributions **must be Roth** (after-tax) — no upfront deduction.
- Magnitude: $24,500 pre-tax at 22% = **$5,390** federal tax deferred per year.

### IRA
- 2026: **$7,500** + **$1,100** catch-up (50+) = **$8,600** (traditional and Roth combined).
- **Roth IRA** contribution phase-out: single **$153K–$168K**; MFJ **$242K–$252K**.
- **Traditional IRA deductibility** (if covered by a workplace plan): single **$81K–$91K**; MFJ **$129K–$149K** (contributing spouse covered). Not covered at work → fully deductible regardless of income.
- **Roth vs. traditional timing:** compare your *marginal rate now* vs. *expected marginal rate in retirement*. Young / low-earning years → Roth (pay 10–12% now, never again). Peak-earning years → traditional (deduct at 22–32%). The worst mistake is contributing to a traditional IRA while covered by a 401(k) above the phase-out — **nondeductible** contributions with none of the Roth's benefits (fix: recharacterize or use backdoor Roth — CPA territory).

## 5. Standard deduction vs. itemizing (2026)

2026 amounts (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): **Single/MFS $16,100 · MFJ $32,200 · HoH $24,150**. Additional **$2,050** per condition for 65+ or blind. Roughly 90% of filers take the standard deduction.

**Itemize only if the total beats it.** The realistic components:
- **SALT: capped at $40,400** for 2026 (phases out from $505K MAGI) — state income/property tax.
- Mortgage interest (on up to $750K of acquisition debt).
- **Charitable:** NEW 2026 — itemizers deduct only the amount **above 0.5% of AGI** (e.g., $500 floor at $100K AGI; the disallowed part carries forward). Top-bracket (37%) filers face a **2/37 haircut** — deductions effectively worth only 35%.
- Medical expenses above **7.5% of AGI**.
- **Bunching strategy:** concentrate two years of charitable giving + elective medical into one tax year to clear the standard-deduction hurdle, then take the standard deduction the next year.
- **New 2026 (permanent):** non-itemizers get an above-the-line charitable deduction — **$1,000** single / **$2,000** MFJ, **cash gifts only**, no AGI floor. (~90M taxpayers used the $300 version in 2020–21; hardly anyone tracks small donations now — the app should remind them to.)
- Stacking on top of *either* choice: the new **$6,000 senior deduction** (§6).

## 6. The four new Schedule 1-A deductions (2025–2028 only)

Created by the OBBBA on **new Schedule 1-A** (IRS guidance IR-2026-28). All are **below-the-line deductions** (reduce taxable income, not AGI), available whether you itemize or not. Married taxpayers **must file jointly** for tips, overtime, and senior deductions — MFS is barred from all three. None are exclusions: the income is still reported and still subject to **FICA**.

| Deduction | Cap | Phase-out (MAGI) | Key gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Qualified tips** | $25,000/return (not doubled for joint) | $150K single / $300K joint; −$100 per $1,000 over | Occupation customarily tipped as of 12/31/2024; valid SSN |
| **Qualified overtime** | $12,500 / **$25,000** joint (doubled) | $150K / $300K; −$100 per $1,000 over (gone at $275K/$550K) | **Premium ("half") only** — not the whole OT check; FLSA-required OT only; W-2 Box 12 code TT from 2026 |
| **Auto loan interest** | $10,000/return | ⚠️ phase-out threshold not verified — check Schedule 1-A instructions | **New** vehicle, **U.S.-assembled**; lender reports on Form 1098-VLI |
| **Senior bonus** | **$6,000**/person 65+ ($12,000 if both spouses qualify) | $75K single / $150K joint; −6% per $1 over (gone at $175K / $350K) | 65+ by 12/31; valid SSN; stacks on standard deduction |

Common misunderstandings to kill in product copy: "no tax on tips/overtime" does **not** mean the income disappears — it's a deduction claimed at filing (withholding still comes out of paychecks all year; adjust the W-4 via the Estimator instead of waiting). The overtime deduction on a $34/hr nurse working 200 OT hours is ~**$3,400 of premium**, not the $10,200 paycheck — worth ~$750 at the 22% bracket, not "thousands."

## 8. Where a CPA / Enrolled Agent is needed (do not productize as DIY)

Multi-state income · AMT exposure · ISOs/NSOs/RSUs · rental property / depreciation · S-corp reasonable salary · backdoor/mega-backdoor Roth · anything involving the 37% bracket haircut, QBI phase-ins, or NIIT · amended returns for missed credits (generally 3 years to claim a refund).

## 9. Flagged uncertainties (do not present as settled)
1. **ACTC 2026:** $1,700 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32; corroborated by 3 sources). One secondary source says $1,800 — treated as wrong.
2. **Senior deduction phase-out endpoint:** $175K single / $350K joint at 6% reduction (2 sources + arithmetic). One source says $150K/$250K — treated as wrong (likely conflated with the one-spouse joint case).
3. **EITC tables:** one secondary source published materially different 2026 limits; discarded in favor of Rev. Proc. 2025-32 figures corroborated by 4+ sources.
4. **Auto-loan-interest phase-out:** cap ($10K) and U.S.-assembly rule verified; MAGI phase-out threshold **not verified** — check current Schedule 1-A instructions before shipping.
5. **1099 reporting threshold $2,000:** single-sourced (OBBBA change) — verify before shipping.
6. **Saver's Match (2027):** announced under SECURE 2.0, Treasury guidance still in progress — describe as "coming," not as claimable.
7. State conformity varies widely (most states have not conformed to the tips/overtime deductions) — federal-only framing required.
